## CL-D130 — A merge conflict or a branch behind its base stops a run before its first gate
**Clauses:** CL-D130-record, CL-D130-driver, CL-D130-forward, CL-D130-tests

*Decision ID:* CL-D130
*Kind:* contract
*Target and revision:* `tetsuh/pi-tidd-agents#330` at its body
*Question:* Both drivers read the pull request's `mergeable_state` only in the final policy, after every gate, although each takes a snapshot before its first gate. A merge conflict or a head behind its base needs a new head, so a run that starts on one spends every gate on a head that cannot become ready (#330). Does a run act on mergeability before its first gate?
*Options and trade-offs:* Stopping before the first gate saves every gate on a head that cannot become ready, at the cost of one more stop. Leaving it to the final policy keeps one place for mergeability, at the cost of every gate's result on such a head.
*Recommendation:* Stop before the first gate.
*Owner choice:* When the snapshot taken before the first gate reports `mergeable_state` `dirty` or `behind`, both drivers stop the run `BLOCKED` before any gate is launched; the reason names the state, and the next action is that the author resolves the merge conflict or updates the branch, then starts a fresh run. The check is in `collectSnapshotEvidence` in `skills/closed-loop-pr/driver/phases.js` and applies while the run's gate log is empty. Any other state, a null or `unknown` one while GitHub computes it and `blocked` included, proceeds, and the final policy is unchanged.
*Rationale:* A gate reviews a head; `dirty` and `behind` say this head will not be merged as it stands, so its gate results are spent. A null or `unknown` state is GitHub still computing and says nothing yet, and `blocked` is for a human to settle (CL-D100). After the first gate, the final policy still decides, so a state that changes during a run is judged where it was before.
*Validity and invalidation conditions:* Applies to the snapshot both packaged drivers take before their first gate. Stopping on another state, or on mergeability after the first gate, requires a new owner decision.
