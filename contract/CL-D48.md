## CL-D48 — The authority headroom property is asserted at the raise
**Clauses:** CL-D48-guard, CL-D48-tests

*Decision ID:* CL-D48
*Kind:* contract
*Target and revision:* `tetsuh/pi-tidd-agents#87` at its initial body and the owner's live instruction to proceed
*Question:* CL-D43's headroom guard asserts `128000 - total > 8000` against the live measurement. Should that property be checked against live growth, or against the raise it was written to qualify?
*Options and trade-offs:* Keeping the live form preserves a guard that has already stopped three consecutive pull requests from writing ordinary prose, and would stop the next ones — #80, #81, #82, and #86 each need authority text and none can be written. Removing the property entirely would let a future raise be taken without leaving room, which is the defect CL-D43 recorded. Asserting the property against the measurement taken when the raise was decided keeps exactly what CL-D43 meant and nothing more.
*Recommendation:* Assert the property at the raise; leave the ceiling as the sole live six-file aggregate limit, with the disclosure and addendum subset guards unchanged.
*Owner choice:* As recommended. The guard now asserts `128000 - RAISE_BASELINE_BYTES > 8000` where `RAISE_BASELINE_BYTES` is 114,563, the six-file measurement at `c2ad0db` when CL-D43 raised the ceiling. The ceiling is the sole live six-file aggregate assertion, and no further live margin is asserted against that total: a margin asserted live would be a new floor of the form this decision removes. The subset guards are untouched and remain live — the 57,160-byte disclosure guard and the CL-D30 addendum guard, 28,000 bytes when this decision was taken and 32,000 since CL-D85, still bound their own scopes, so prose landing in `autofix.md` is bounded by the disclosure guard rather than by the aggregate. This decision restores aggregate room only.
*Rationale:* The constant form conflated two different claims: that a raise leaves real room when taken, and that the graph has not since grown into the margin. Only the first was intended, and as a live assertion the second **became the effective ceiling** at 120,000 — invisibly, because no decision ever set that number. Three pull requests paid for it: #72 trimmed prose to fit, #77 trimmed twice and landed at 119,999, and #85 overran by nine bytes and shortened a shared-contract sentence to compensate. That is precisely the pattern CL-D43 exists to prevent, a guard becoming a term in every unrelated feature's negotiation, reappearing one level below the guard CL-D43 fixed. A raise-time baseline is a historical fact that cannot drift, so the property stays checkable forever without ever binding ordinary work. Scope is the aggregate guard alone, under owner decision `DEC-PR88-SCOPE-001` choice B: Issue #87's `autofix.md` split and its full destination-capacity proof stay open there, and the measured reason is recorded with them — at the reviewed head the disclosure guard leaves 1,714 bytes for `autofix.md`, so a decision-sized addition is not guaranteed to fit in the file where decisions actually land. This decision does not raise the ceiling and does not weaken the reasons to avoid raising it again: at 44,895 bytes `autofix.md` has grown a further 4,060 bytes since CL-D43 named it, and splitting `autofix.md` remains the recorded structural answer to sustained growth, unchanged by this correction. Splitting is also not a substitute for a ceiling, since a split file stays inside the measured set; it bounds how much prose any one file carries, not how much the graph carries.
*Validity and invalidation conditions:* Applies to the six-file authority guard only. The 128,000-byte ceiling, the CL-D30 addendum guard, the disclosure guard, and the packaged-helper alarms are unchanged. A future raise must record its own baseline measurement and satisfy the same property at that moment. Reintroducing any live assertion that bounds the total below the ceiling requires a new owner decision, as does raising the ceiling itself.
