## CL-D43 — Authority byte guards are set once, with headroom
**Clauses:** CL-D43-guards, CL-D43-tests

*Decision ID:* CL-D43
*Kind:* contract
*Target and revision:* `tetsuh/pi-tidd-agents#73` at its initial body and the owner's live instruction to raise the numbers with real margin
*Question:* The six-file authority ceiling is spent. Should it be raised, funded by a trim, or left to bind each feature in turn, and by how much?
*Options and trade-offs:* Option A leaves the guards where they are, which makes the next decision's prose the guard's problem and repeats the pattern of deciding a raise inside an unrelated feature. Option B funds the shortfall by a second duplication harvest, which Issue #58 already did once and which trades reviewed prose for bytes. Option C raises the two binding guards once, generously, and records what they protect. A costs nothing now and recurs; B risks weakening prose to buy space; C spends the argument once.
*Recommendation:* Option C, raising both binding guards and leaving the disclosure guard alone.
*Owner choice:* Option C. The six authority files move from 116,000 to 128,000 bytes and the CL-D30 addendum from 25,022 to 28,000. CL-D74 later reset the addendum guard to 29,000 bytes for the pinned cleanup mode, on the same terms; CL-D85 later reset it to 32,000 bytes and the authority ceiling to 156,000 bytes on the same terms. The pre-split disclosure guard stays at 57,160, because 57,160 is not a budget: it encodes the claim that progressive disclosure is smaller than the monolith it replaced, so raising it would falsify what it exists to prove. Both raised guards assert the live measurement, and their recorded figures are revision-qualified rather than maintained running totals.
*Rationale:* The Issue #24 baseline began at 99,182 bytes; CL-D34 raised it to 108,000, CL-D36 to 112,000, and CL-D39 to 116,000. Every one of those was decided inside the feature that needed the space, and a raise decided inside a feature pull request makes the guard a term in a negotiation about an unrelated change and hands that feature's reviewers an argument they did not ask for; PR #71 carried such a raise and PR #72 avoided one only by trimming to 61 bytes of headroom. Recent decisions cost roughly 1,500 to 2,000 bytes each, so the previous 4,000-byte raises bought about two decisions apiece and the argument recurred every second feature. Twelve thousand bytes buys about six. The addendum is raised with it because at 23,677 of 25,022 bytes it had 1,345 left and would simply have become the binding guard one pull request later. This raise is not a licence to grow: the guards exist to keep the reviewed authority graph small enough to read, and the effective room for `autofix.md` prose remains the disclosure guard's margin, not the six-file ceiling, because most new prose lands there. At 40,835 bytes `autofix.md` is the largest authority file and absorbs each new decision, so the next structural answer is splitting `autofix.md` the way the PR Skill split produced the 57,160 baseline, not a further raise.
*Validity and invalidation conditions:* Applies to the two raised guards only. It weakens no assertion into a warning, trims no prose, and changes no workflow behaviour, agent, or helper. A further raise of either guard, or any raise of the disclosure guard, requires a new owner decision and should be treated as evidence that the split named above is overdue instead. If a duplication harvest later recovers substantial space, the guards should be lowered toward the measurement rather than left wide. CL-D48 later re-expressed this record's headroom property: it is asserted against the measurement taken at this raise rather than against the live total, because as a live assertion the margin had become an unstated second ceiling.
