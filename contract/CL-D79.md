## CL-D79 — The post-push guard accepts a sole-child chain of pushes
**Clauses:** CL-D79-definition, CL-D79-autofix, CL-D79-contract, CL-D79-record-keeping, CL-D79-readme, CL-D79-record, CL-D79-tests

*Decision ID:* CL-D79
*Kind:* contract
*Target and revision:* `tetsuh/pi-tidd-agents#140` at its body and the owner choice https://github.com/tetsuh/pi-tidd-agents/issues/140#issuecomment-5741456347 (working session of 2026-09-19)
*Question:* The exact-autofix grant allows up to five correction pushes per run, but `operator_revalidate`'s post-push guard accepted only a head whose sole parent is the captured operator baseline. The operator checkout does not move during a run, so a second push is the baseline's grandchild; PR #139's autofix run published its second correction and then ended `BLOCKED`. What should the guard accept after a later push?
*Options and trade-offs:* Option A has the run pass the heads it pushed before the current one, oldest first, and the guard accept the chain only when every link is a sole-parent step and the chain starts at the baseline and ends at the tracking ref; it adds one optional input to two frozen CLI operations (CL-D44), which is why this is a decision. Option B re-captures the operator baseline after each push, which changes what `OPERATOR_CHECKOUT@H` means for every later guard. Option C states one published correction per run and drops the five-push cap.
*Recommendation:* Option A.
*Owner choice:* Option A (https://github.com/tetsuh/pi-tidd-agents/issues/140#issuecomment-5741456347). `operator_revalidate` and `build_operator_revalidate` take an optional `priorPushHeads`, an array of the commit OIDs the run pushed before `postPushHead`, oldest first, and never without `postPushHead`. The guard accepts the chain `[...priorPushHeads, postPushHead]` only when it holds at most five distinct 40-hex OIDs, the first is the sole child of the captured baseline head, each later one is the sole child of the one before it, read without replace objects, and the last is the current tracking ref; everything else the baseline compares is unchanged. A single-push run sends the request it sent before. The guard proves ancestry, not who pushed: any chain meeting those conditions passes it, whoever pushed it, as a single sole child meeting the same conditions did before this record. That the chain is the run's own is not something the guard or any other check establishes (owner choice, https://github.com/tetsuh/pi-tidd-agents/issues/140#issuecomment-5742362840, restated at https://github.com/tetsuh/pi-tidd-agents/issues/140#issuecomment-5742591079). `WORKSPACE_POST_PUSH(C, O)` and `AUTOFIX_WORKSPACE@C` bind only the current head to the run's own workspace, and the earlier heads rest on the parent's record of its pushes, which no check enforced when this decision was taken. CL-D86 later took that decision: the heads are derived by `build_operator_revalidate` from the run's own post-push snapshots, so the parent supplies none by hand. A linear child someone else pushed, supplied in the chain, is accepted by the helper: a known residual, not a defect; binding every head to a packaged record would need a new owner decision.
*Rationale:* The parent names the chain from its own record of what it pushed, so a merge, a reordering, or a head the record leaves out breaks it, as the sole-child rule broke the second push; the baseline keeps meaning the checkout the run started from, and the five-push cap in the grant becomes reachable.
*Validity and invalidation conditions:* Applies to the post-push form of `operator_revalidate` and its builder. The immutable baseline, the clone form, and every pre-push boundary are unchanged. Re-capturing the baseline after a push, or binding every chain head to a packaged record of the run's pushes, requires a new owner decision; CL-D86 is that decision for the derivation of the heads, and leaves this guard's own refusals unchanged.
